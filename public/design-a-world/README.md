# Design a World

A turn-based planet game for gifted 5th graders. Open `index.html` in a browser and play. It does not need an account, a PIN, or the math and science pages.

Earth is the first world: real continents, oceans, and mountain ranges, with climate that follows latitude. Pick a region to manage, such as Texas and the Great Plains, the Amazon, the Sahel, or the Arctic coast. The main view is a spinning globe in space, with room around it for stars and the glow of the air. Day and night, soft clouds, ice caps, and city lights are on that globe. A Region map is the zoomed place you edit. Planet tools (core heat, CO2, oxygen, albedo, rain, uplift, sea level) show on the globe within a few seasons: coasts, ice, volcano glow, and the color of the air. The climate meter reads Ice Age, Cool, Cozy, Hot, or Scorching, and it does not jump a whole band from one button.

Students shape land, water, living things, and nations. Each season updates climate, biomes, the food web, resources, and the towns. A log says why the numbers moved. Charts keep the history. Nimbus, the map guide, sits beside the tools. Science cards show up every few seasons, fit on the screen, and can be skipped.

Nothing here assigns a grade or a point total. The Notebook is evidence for the teacher: choices, written reasons, and what the world did.

Scenarios disagree with each other on purpose. A dam, a herd, a coal mine, and a road through a wetland each help something and harm something else.
