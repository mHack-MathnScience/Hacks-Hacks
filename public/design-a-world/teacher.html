Paste one or more turn-in codes (one per line), or open save files (.json). You can select many files at once. Everything stays in this browser; nothing is uploaded.

About the check number. Every code and save file carries a check number (a checksum) made from the student’s work. If the work is changed by hand or damaged when copying, the check number will not match, and this page shows ✗ Changed.

This is tamper-resistant, not secure. It catches accidents and casual edits, but a determined person could make a new valid code. Use it with your normal classroom judgment.

Class list

History events: teacher notes and preview

History events come from School Bot’s history_events.json. Students meet them when their world is ready (for example, after the first town is built). Every choice is peaceful civic action; there is never an option to enslave, buy, or sell people, to profit from slavery, or to fight. War appears only as causes, perspectives, and costs, and imagery is symbolic. Each choice’s text is checked by an automated scan when the content is imported and again in the game.

For other computers, share this link instead: (the game also has a History events switch in Settings).

Civilizations (god game): grade pack

Students shape land, climate and biomes; settlements grow on their own where the geography supports them, and problems arrive that are tied to the social studies TEKS for the grade you pick. Students answer with a policy, by reshaping the land, or by writing a document, then watch the consequences unfold and look back. There are no scores: you see choices, writing and reflections. Every option is peaceful civic action; slavery appears only as a violation of human rights, and no one is hurt in any event.

For other computers, share this link: . A world keeps the pack it started with.

Challenge level

Challenge is the default (GT): a smaller starting budget and fewer world-shaping changes per era, competing needs, open-ended problems justified with claim, evidence and reasoning, "Show me" only after 2 tries or a written attempt, predictions, and consequence chains. Support gives a bigger budget and more changes, shorter writing minimums, and the original "Show me" timing. Neither mode is graded.

For one student on any computer, share: . The link only sets the level of a new world. Each world keeps its level once it is made, so students cannot switch by editing the link. Changing this setting updates worlds on this computer the next time they open, and the report notes the change.

Fun features: teacher switches

For other computers, share this link: . Nature surprises are gentle (no one is hurt), framed as a world need, and give no grade or TEKS credit. Students can also turn them off in Settings.

Sound is off by default; students turn it on with the speaker button. Reduced motion is in Settings and follows the computer’s reduce-motion setting. Achievements are play-only: they are never shown in this view, the Turn-in report, PDF, image, slideshow, or CSV.

Favorites rule: each stage, students star 2 Field Guide entries (or fewer if they have fewer) and write a short explanation (12+ words) for each. Correct picks count as evidence; written explanations make it strong.
